2. Purpose and function of Dogs Out

Dogs Out is a digital platform that puts dog owners in touch with each other and with people who look after dogs.

In particular, Dogs Out lets you:

  • create a profile for yourself and for each of your dogs;
  • browse dog owners nearby and match with them ("Discover");
  • exchange messages with your matches, and invite them for a walk;
  • create and join meetups in a park ("Playdates"), each with its own group chat;
  • tell your matches that you are out walking, at the park or otherwise available, and see theirs ("Who's outside");
  • offer to look after dogs or look for someone to look after yours, post and accept dogsitting jobs, share the details of a sitting, and review a sitter afterwards ("Dogsitting");
  • raise an alert when your dog has gone missing ("SOS");
  • find dog shelters near you ("Shelters");
  • and use further features that make meeting other dog owners and organising dog care easier.

Dogs Out puts people in touch. Dogs Out does not itself provide dogsitting, dog walking or any other care for animals, and it does not process payments between users.

In particular, Dogs Out is:

  • not the employer of any sitter;

  • and not responsible for how a meeting, a walk, a playdate or a sitting is actually carried out.

Any agreement to look after a dog is made solely between the owner and the sitter.

8.1 Roles

You can mark yourself as available to look after dogs ("sitter"), as looking for someone to look after yours ("owner"), or both. Each role only gives access to the other side's list: sitters see owners who are looking, owners see sitters who are available.

8.2 Jobs and offers

Owners can post a dogsitting job with the dates, times and dogs concerned. Sitters can offer to take a job. A job is only taken once the owner has accepted a sitter's offer.

Contact, a job post, an offer or an informal agreement in a chat does not on its own establish any agreement between the parties.

8.3 The agreement between owner and sitter

Any agreement to look after a dog is made solely between the owner and the sitter. Dogs Out is not a party to it.

The parties are themselves responsible for agreeing on everything that matters for the sitting before it begins, in particular:

  • the date, start and end of the sitting;
  • where it takes place, and the handover of keys;
  • the dogs concerned, their feeding, medication and health;
  • their behaviour, including towards other dogs, children and strangers;
  • what to do in an emergency, and which vet to contact;
  • any compensation;
  • and any other arrangements.

We strongly recommend a personal meeting and a trial meet-and-greet with the dog before a first sitting.

8.4 Handover details

Once a sitter has been accepted, the owner can share the address, an emergency phone number and a to-do list with that sitter. These details are shown only to the accepted sitter, for that sitting. The sitter may use them only to carry out the sitting.

8.5 Cancellations

A sitter who can no longer carry out a sitting must cancel it in the app as early as possible; the job is then opened again for other sitters. A cancellation less than 24 hours before the start counts as late. After three late cancellations within twelve months, the sitter cannot take new jobs for 30 days.

8.6 Compensation

Dogs Out does not set, collect or pass on any compensation for a sitting. Whether and how much a sitter is paid is agreed and settled directly between the owner and the sitter. The parties are themselves responsible for any tax and social insurance obligations that may arise from it.

Dogs Out does not check the identity, experience, qualifications, reliability or suitability of sitters or owners, and does not verify any self-declared information. The choice of a sitter, and the decision to accept an offer, is the owner's alone. Each sitter likewise decides alone which jobs they take on.

9. SOS alerts for lost dogs

If a dog you own or are responsible for has gone missing, you can raise an SOS alert. The alert is sent to every Dogs Out user within 100 km of the place where the dog went missing, with the dog's name and photos, the place, your name and a note if you add one. Anyone who receives it can open a chat with you.

You may raise an alert only for a dog that is genuinely missing. You must close the alert as soon as the dog has been found or the alert is no longer needed. You may raise no more than three alerts per week.

Dogs Out does not search for missing dogs and does not guarantee that an alert reaches anyone or leads to the dog being found. We recommend also informing the national dog database, the local animal shelters, the police and your vet.

Misuse of the SOS feature, in particular a false alarm raised deliberately, may lead to the suspension of your account.
